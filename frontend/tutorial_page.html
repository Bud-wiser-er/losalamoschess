    Lesson 1: Introduction

    Los Alamos Chess is a simplified version of chess on a 6×6 board, invented in 1956 for early chess computers.

    Lesson 2: Board Setup & Pieces

    Each player has 1 King, 1 Queen, 2 Rooks, 2 Knights, and 6 Pawns. Bishops are not used.

    Lesson 3: The Pawn

    • Moves forward one square
    • Captures diagonally only if an enemy piece is there
    • No double-step, no en passant
    • Promotion on the 6th rank

    Lesson 4: The Rook

    • Moves any number of squares
    • Can only move vertically or horizontally

    Lesson 5: The Knight

    • Moves in an "L" shape
    • Can jump over other pieces

    Lesson 6: The Queen

    • Combines rook and diagonal power
    • Most powerful piece

    Lesson 7: The King

    • Moves one square in any direction
    • Cannot move into check
    • No castling in Los Alamos Chess

    Lesson 8: Special Rules

    • No bishops: You cannot move diagonally long-range; only queens and pawns control diagonals.
    • No en passant: You cannot capture a pawn that has just moved forward beside your pawn.
    • No pawn double-step: Pawns may only move forward one square, never two.
    • No castling: You cannot move king and rook together; kings must find safety by walking.
    • Promotion on rank 6: Pawns that reach the last row must promote (usually to a queen).
